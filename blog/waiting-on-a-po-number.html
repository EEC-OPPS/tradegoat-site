Somebody Is Standing at a Parts Counter Waiting on You

By Travis Evans, Founder of TradeGOAT · 20+ years in the trades

My name is Travis Evans and I own Evans Elevator Company in Crandall, Texas. A good part of my week used to be spent as a middleman between a man on a job and a man behind a counter. He calls me, tells me what he needs and who he is standing in front of, and then waits while I find a number and read it back to him. It is maybe four minutes of actual work and it costs a good deal more than four minutes.

The way it usually goes wrong is ordinary.

He calls while you are under a car and you tell him you will call him right back, and then you do not, because two other things happen first.

He gets the number written on the back of a receipt, and by the time anybody in the office hears about that order it has already been delivered and billed.

The counter will not release the part without a PO number, so a man who is otherwise ready to work stands there.

Two people call the same supply house about the same job and two numbers get issued for one order.

The part shows up three weeks later on a vendor statement and nobody can say for certain which job it belongs on.

That last one is the expensive one. The four minutes on the phone are annoying. A part that never lands on a job is money that quietly leaves the company, and you find out about it at the end of the year when the job you thought made twenty percent made eleven.

The ask goes in from where he is standing

In TradeGOAT a tech has a Parts request on his field screen, sitting alongside adding a receipt, starting a new ticket, and looking at his schedule. Inside a service ticket it is on the ticket itself, because the ticket already knows the job and there is nothing to pick.

The card asks him three things and only the first one is required. What do you need, in his own words, the way he would say it over the phone. One of ours might type that he needs forty feet of three quarter EMT and a bender. Who from, if he already knows, and he can pick a vendor you have used before or type in a new one. For which job, if he is not already on one.

Then there is a checkbox that says I need a PO number. That checkbox is the whole thing, and I will come back to it.

There is no amount field anywhere on his side of this, and no dollar sign on any of it. When a man is calling for a PO he usually does not know what the stuff costs yet, and asking him to guess would put a made-up number into your job costs. The price catches up later, on the vendor, and I will come back to that too.

The office answers on the screen it already has open

Every job page in TradeGOAT has a panel called From the field. Notes, schedule-change asks and parts requests from your crew land there. Until recently that panel was something you read. Now it has a button on it.

A request that asked for a PO number offers Issue a PO number. One click. A request that did not ask for one offers Mark handled, for the times somebody just needed the office to go get something. When a number has been issued, the row offers Mark delivered, and that is the end of it.

Those are the only buttons that exist. A row that is finished does not keep offering anything, which is the practical reason two numbers stop getting issued for one ask. Requests that came in without a job attached collect on their own list at Open parts requests, and your Jobs screen tells you how many are waiting on you whenever any are.

The number goes back to his phone

The moment you issue it, his phone gets it. PO 2026-0142, Bearing Supply. You are clear to order. He reads it to the counterman and drives out with the part. When the office marks it delivered, he is told that too.

Those two moments are the only two that reach his phone. Nothing else about a parts request buzzes anybody. That is deliberate and it is written as a rule rather than left to whoever builds the next screen, because a phone that goes off for every small status change is a phone the man turns notifications off on, and then the one message that actually mattered never gets seen.

The part I would want to know: the number he gets is your number. PO numbering runs off the same Settings screen your proposals do, with your prefix and your padding, so your purchase orders and your proposals read like they came from the same company. That screen shows you what the next PO number will be. There is no second numbering scheme to set up.

If your shop does not use POs, you will never see the word

Plenty of good shops have never issued a purchase order and never will. The tech says what he needs, somebody in the office orders it, and that is the whole system.

That checkbox on his card is the only opt-in there is. If nobody ticks it, no purchase-order verb appears anywhere. The office row offers Mark handled and nothing else. You are not carrying a feature you did not ask for around on a screen you open forty times a day.

The price catches up on the vendor

When the invoice comes and you know what the part actually cost, that number goes on the job's cost sheet, under the vendor it came from. That is the same place every other cost on that job is entered, and it is the only place a parts cost is ever written.

The parts request itself never grows a dollar amount of its own. That was on purpose. Two numbers for one part, sitting in two different places, is how a job's cost becomes something you have to reconcile instead of something you can read. The request records the ask and who it went to. The money lives on the vendor, on the job, where the rest of the money is.

Which means the part he picked up on a Monday morning ends up counted against that job, and the profit number you look at later is one you can trust. That is the same idea as knowing whether you made money on the job, and parts are usually the piece that leaks.

What it looks like on a Monday

Your tech is at a building at nine and finds he is short on conduit. He opens Parts request on his phone, types what he needs, picks Bearing Supply from the list, ticks the box for a PO number, and sends it. He goes back to work.

You are at your desk with that job open anyway. The From the field panel has his ask on it. You click Issue a PO number. His phone gets 2026-0142 and the vendor name, and he heads to the counter.

Friday the invoice comes in at three hundred and eighty dollars. It goes on that job under Bearing Supply. Nobody had to remember anything, and the job's cost went up by exactly what the part cost.

Straight about where this stands

This is in the tool now. The request card is on the field screen and on both service ticket screens, the buttons are on the job's From the field panel, and PO numbering is on your Settings numbering screen next to your proposal numbering.

Two things it does not do, so you know before you set it up. It does not do receiving in the warehouse sense, meaning there is no counting of what came in against what was ordered. Marking a request delivered is a person saying the part is here, not an inventory count. And it does not talk to your supply house. Somebody still calls or walks in and places the order. What this changes is that he is not waiting on you to do it, and the order does not disappear on its way to your books.
