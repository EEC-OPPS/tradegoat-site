Won vs Lost: What Your Winning Proposals Have in Common

By Travis Evans, Founder of TradeGOAT · 20+ years in the trades

For years I remembered the jobs we lost by how they felt. That one was too high. That GC always goes cheap. What I never had was the numbers from the jobs we won sitting right next to the numbers from the jobs we didn't.

In TradeGOAT, click Reports in the left menu, then Won vs Lost. It lines up your winning proposals next to the ones that got away.

The top of the page

Four numbers across the top:

  • Won: how many proposals you won, and what they were worth.
  • Lost: how many you lost, and what they were worth.
  • Win rate: out of the proposals that got decided.
  • Left on the table: the value of the proposals you lost.

Under those, a few short lines call out what stands out in your own numbers.

Your winning shape next to your losing shape

This is the part I look at most. Each row shows the jobs you won in one column and the jobs you lost in the other:

  • Average proposal and Typical (median) proposal
  • Target margin: what the calculator was set to
  • Price per crew-day
  • Days on site
  • Crew size
  • Parts share of price: material-heavy jobs next to labor-heavy ones

Price per crew-day is the one I'd start with. Two proposals can land at the same total and be completely different jobs. When the price is split across the days your crew is actually out there, you can see whether the ones you lost were priced harder per day than the ones you won, or about the same. If they're about the same, price probably wasn't the reason.

Where you win

Two more sections break it down:

  • By proposal size: your win rate at each size of proposal.
  • By job type: which kinds of work you win, and which ones you mostly price for somebody else.

If you win most of your small service work and almost none of your big jobs, that's worth knowing before you spend another weekend on a big bid.

Why you lost, in your own words

When someone marks a proposal Lost, TradeGOAT asks, "Do you know why you lost this job?" Whatever your people answer collects on this page under Why we lost, in your words. After a few months you're looking at a real list, not a feeling.

What makes it work

It only knows what you mark. When a proposal doesn't win, mark it Lost instead of letting it sit. Every one you mark makes the comparison sharper.
